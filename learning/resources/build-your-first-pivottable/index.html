How to Build Your First Excel PivotTable

PivotTables help turn long lists of records into useful summaries. Learn how to prepare the source, choose fields and check the result.

7 min read · Published 2026-11-26 · Boztik Learning Editorial Team

What a PivotTable is for

A PivotTable summarises a larger table without requiring you to write a formula for every group. For example, a sales list with thousands of transactions can be summarised by branch, product, month or salesperson. You can rearrange the fields to explore different questions while leaving the original records in place.

A PivotTable does not repair poor data or decide what a business should do. It organises the records you give it, so the quality of the answer still depends on the source data and the question.

Prepare the source table

Start with one header row, one record per row and one field per column. Give every column a unique name. Remove completely empty rows from the middle of the data and check that dates and numbers are stored in the correct format. Convert the range to a spreadsheet table if your application supports it.

Keep the raw data unchanged. If you need to correct spelling, remove duplicates or standardise dates, do that in a documented cleaning step first.

Create the summary

  1. Select a cell inside the prepared data table.
  2. Choose the PivotTable command in your spreadsheet application.
  3. Choose where the summary should appear, usually a new worksheet for a first attempt.
  4. Drag a category field, such as Branch, into Rows.
  5. Drag a numeric field, such as Amount, into Values.
  6. Check whether the result uses Sum or Count as intended. A text or incorrectly formatted number field may be counted rather than added.

Exact menu names vary between Excel versions and other spreadsheet applications, but the field layout idea is similar.

Ask a useful question with fields

Suppose your table contains OrderDate, Branch, Product, Quantity and Amount. To answer “Which branch generated the most revenue?”, put Branch in Rows and Amount in Values, summarised by Sum. To compare product demand, put Product in Rows and Quantity in Values. To see monthly revenue, group a valid date field by month where the tool supports it.

Use filters sparingly and label the result so another person can understand what is included.

Refresh and validate

A PivotTable may not automatically include new records added after it was created. Refresh it after changing the source data, and confirm the source range or table includes the new rows. Microsoft documents the refresh process for Excel across desktop and mobile versions.

Before sharing a result, compare the PivotTable grand total with an independent total from the source data. Check that filters have not excluded records and that blank or unexpected categories have not been overlooked.

Avoid common interpretation mistakes

  • Do not assume a high total means strong performance without considering volume, time period or context.
  • Do not compare groups with different date ranges as if they were equivalent.
  • Do not confuse Count with Sum.
  • Do not forget to refresh after adding or changing source records.
  • Do not hide inconvenient categories without explaining why.
