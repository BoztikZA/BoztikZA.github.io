Quick guides for the image workbench

1. Remove a small blemish or mark
  1. Choose Spot repair.
  2. Select Set sample point, then click a clean area with similar texture near the mark.
  3. Paint gently over the blemish in short strokes. Set a new sample if the texture or lighting changes.
  4. Use Undo if the repair repeats texture unnaturally. This clone brush is best for small marks, not large object removal.
2. Remove a background
  1. Choose Background cutout and load the optional model. The first download is about 32 MB plus the runtime.
  2. Click the subject you want to keep. Add more points on the subject or switch to remove-area mode and click background areas.
  3. Choose PNG when exporting to preserve transparency.

The model runs on your device after download. It is an interactive selection aid, not a perfect one-click cutout; inspect hair and fine edges.

3. Reduce mild image noise
  1. Use the Noise reduction slider to set a low starting value.
  2. Apply the filter once, then inspect fine detail at 100%.
  3. Undo if skin, hair or texture becomes too smooth.

This is a lightweight edge-aware smoothing filter, not a specialist AI denoiser.

4. Crop, rotate and export
  1. Choose Crop and drag over the part of the image you want to keep.
  2. Choose a preset ratio if needed, then apply the crop. Rotate in 90-degree steps if required.
  3. Export as JPG for compact photos, PNG for transparency, or WebP for supported websites.
  4. Keep your original file. The editor creates a separate download.

The workbench currently edits a browser-sized working copy of up to 6 megapixels. It is intended for everyday web and social use, not high-resolution print mastering.

Build a non-destructive workflow

Keep the original untouched, use adjustment layers for tonal and colour work, convert reusable source layers to Smart Objects, and name important groups. Save a working PSD/PSB before exporting flattened copies.

Retouch skin without removing texture

Start with restrained healing and clone work on a separate layer. Correct uneven tone locally, zoom out often, and inspect at 100%. Frequency separation can help with isolated tone or texture issues, but it is easy to overuse and create waxy skin.

Improve a difficult mask

Separate the subject from the background with a clean initial selection, then refine hair and soft edges at a realistic zoom. Check the mask against light and dark backgrounds. Avoid excessive feathering; it creates halos.

Choose an export format

Use JPEG for photographic images when a compact file matters, PNG when transparency or crisp flat graphics matter, and WebP when the destination supports it. Keep an editable master and inspect the exported file at its actual display size.

Prepare an image for print

Decide the final physical size first, then check effective pixel dimensions at that size. 300 ppi is a common close-view print target, not a universal rule. Ask the print provider for specifications before resampling.

Make colour adjustments feel natural

Correct white balance and exposure before adding a look. Protect skin tones, watch saturated highlights, and compare against the original. A strong grade should support the subject rather than become the subject.

Sharpen with restraint

Reduce distracting noise before final output sharpening. Apply sharpening selectively and check for halos around high-contrast edges. Avoid judging sharpness from a zoomed-out preview alone.

These are general starting points. The right settings depend on the source image, intended output and software version.
