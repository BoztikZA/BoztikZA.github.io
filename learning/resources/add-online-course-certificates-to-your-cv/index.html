How to Add an Online Course Certificate to Your CV

A course certificate can support your CV when you explain what you learned and what you can demonstrate. Here's how to present it accurately.

6 min read · Published 2026-11-26 · Boztik Learning Editorial Team

Use a clear section heading

If you have completed relevant short courses, add a section called “Professional Development”, “Additional Training” or “Relevant Courses”. Place it where it supports the role you are applying for. For an entry-level role, relevant learning may sit above unrelated work history; for an experienced applicant, it may fit below employment and qualifications.

Do not place an independently issued course certificate under a heading that implies a formal degree, licence or accredited qualification.

Write a concise, factual entry

Include the course title, provider, completion month and year, and a verification link if one is available. Add one short line describing the relevant skills or practical work. Keep the wording factual and avoid claims that the course did not assess.

Example
Practical Artificial Intelligence & AI Productivity — Boztik Learning Hub, November 2026
Completed structured training in prompt design, AI-assisted research, output verification and responsible use. Certificate of Achievement: [verification URL].

Replace the example date and skills with your actual completion details. Do not list a course as completed before you have passed its assessment.

Describe evidence, not just attendance

Employers are more likely to understand a skill when you can explain how you used it. Where appropriate, link to a small portfolio item, practical exercise or project that you created yourself. For example, a spreadsheet course could lead to a cleaned sample dataset and a short report; an AI course could lead to a documented workflow with checks for accuracy and privacy.

Never include confidential workplace data, personal information or an employer's intellectual property in a public portfolio.

Be transparent about accreditation

Not every online course is formally accredited. If a provider issues an independent certificate of completion or achievement, describe it that way. Do not call it a diploma, professional certification or accredited qualification unless the relevant awarding body and accreditation status genuinely support that wording.

Boztik courses are independently developed and not formally accredited. Boztik certificates record successful completion of the stated course assessment; they are not academic qualifications, professional licences or a guarantee of employment. This distinction should remain clear on your CV and LinkedIn profile.

Prepare to discuss what you learned

Before an interview, choose one skill from the course and prepare a short example: the problem, the steps you took, the result and what you would improve. Be ready to explain the limits of your knowledge too. A certificate can show structured effort, but your explanation and practical evidence show how you apply the learning.
