About this course

Most spreadsheet problems are not about clever formulas. They are about untidy data, unclear questions and numbers nobody checked. This course teaches you to start with the question, clean the data, calculate with confidence, summarise with PivotTables, chart honestly and write up what you found. You practise on a downloadable café sales file with blank rows, repeated orders and inconsistent spellings, then finish with a practical on a fresh dataset.

Before you start: You should be comfortable using a computer and have access to any spreadsheet program, such as Excel, Google Sheets or LibreOffice Calc; no prior data experience is needed.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain the difference between data and information, and turn a vague request into a question that data can answer
  • Recognise records, fields and data types, and set up a sheet with one record per row
  • Clean a messy dataset by removing blank rows and duplicates and standardising text, while keeping the raw data untouched
  • Write formulas using relative and absolute references, IF, SUMIF, COUNTIF and lookups, and read common error messages
  • Sort, filter and summarise data with PivotTables without corrupting the underlying records
  • Choose a chart that fits the question and avoid designs that mislead
  • Write a short report that gives the answer first and states its caveats
  • Plan a simple one-screen dashboard whose totals stay correct when the data changes

Who it is for

  • Learners who use spreadsheets but have never been taught to structure or clean data properly
  • Small-business owners, freelancers and club treasurers who need to understand their own numbers
  • Students and job seekers who want a practical data skill for admin, finance, marketing or operations roles
  • Anyone preparing for further study in data analytics

What to learn next

  • Learn SQL to ask questions of larger databases than a spreadsheet can comfortably hold
  • Explore Power Query or an equivalent import-and-transform tool to automate the cleaning steps you did by hand
  • Study introductory statistics so you can judge when a difference in your numbers is large enough to matter
  • Try a dedicated dashboard tool such as Power BI, Looker Studio or Tableau once the one-screen principles feel natural
